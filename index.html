Ioannis Ziogas

I'm Ioannis (Giannis) Ziogas - an AI researcher building on the intersection of signal processing, deep learning, and time series in healthcare and beyond. Starting from classical signal processing fundamentals and challenging biomedical applications more than half a decade ago, the domain of self-supervised learning provided fertile ground for a deep and rewarding research journey during my PhD studies, where I worked on the frontier of the intersection between self-supervised learning and signal processing principles. Moving forward, I am interested in further integrating signal processing priors into deep learning systems towards enhanced solutions in the challenging domains of biomedical signals, acoustics, healthcare, NLP, and beyond.

Research

AI research progress in terms of novel and smarter techniques has plateaued by relying on scaling data and parameters. Promoting structure in representations through strong inductive biases is a paradigm that brings collective improvements over all representational aspects. I work across every step of the theoretical design to final application pipeline - conceiving the idea, deriving the mathematical and experimental signal processing and deep learning formulations, designing and implementing architecture, data processing, pre-training, post-training usage scenarios, post-analysis of representation properties, insights through detailed visualizations.

Featured -- 2026

Omni-modal & Full-stack Representations for Time Series

Learning structured representations for time series data with arbitrarily large (omni-modal) number of channels and modalities, to perform full-stack representation learning tasks: disentanglement, prediction, data synthesis. Omni-modal decomposition autoencoders as full-stack representation learners, powered by self-supervised decomposition, and wearable biomedical applications (human activity recognition, identity recognition) that require efficiency and intelligent handling of modalities.

OmniDecVAE self-supervised decomposition learning autoencoders modality scaling wearable representations efficiency
Featured -- 2026 focus

Learnable Signal Processing for Time Series

Classical signal processing techniques utilize expert knowledge encoded into mathematical frameworks, producing static and reproducible representations of signals. Parameterizing these classical algorithms into learnable blocks allows for data-driven discovery and re-definition of algorithms such as signal decomposition, digital filters, wavelets, thus re-defining how signal processing analysis is done. Connecting the domain of representation learning and self-supervision with classical signal processing, with applications in biosignals, acoustics, LLMs, computer vision.

learnable time-frequency analysis parameterized fourier transform latent signal processing signal processing layers non-stationary signals synthetic data
Featured -- 2025

Advancing Disentangled Representation Learning

Integrating the dynamics of signal decomposition into variational autoencoders to improve disentanglement in representations. Self-supervised latent decomposition learns a disentangled representation by achieving equilibrium by contrasting fundamental components of a signal. (Demonstrated through applications in speech recognition, dysarthric speech and speech emotion recognition.)

DecVAE self-supervised unsupervised latent decomposition variational decomposition learning disentanglement structure

Self-Supervised Learning

Integrating structural biases into self-supervised learning workflows, adapting existing successful workflows through signal processing - CochCepsAugment augmentation inspired from SpecAugment and SimCLR, inserting the decomposition operation as masking into wav2vec2.0, graph masking operations.

CochCepsAugment decomposition masking graph neural networks contrastive learning augmentations

Applications in Health Informatics

Utilizing representations as biomarkers for improved performance in healthcare informatics analysis - activity recognition, biometrics, affective computing, neurodegenerative diseases (Parkinson's, ALS) and motor/non-motor symptoms (tremor, dysarthria, gastrointestinal) detection, neurological disorders and comorbidities.

health informatics prediction EEG inertial sensors wearables smartphone data electrogastrogram migraine bioacoustics

Speech Recognition

Investigating speech at the lowest levels of spectral properties and combinations of formants that form speech units - simulating speech vowels, through artificial speakers to analyze disentanglement in speech data. Utilizing disentangled representations to interpret how separating these factors of variation contributes to classic speech recognition tasks i.e. speaker identification, phoneme recognition in real speech.

simulated speech speaker identification phoneme recognition speech decomposition

Signal processing & Representation Learning for Material Analysis

Comparing different sensing materials and isolating material signatures on physiological multi-lead waveforms - using heartbeat segmentation, unsupervised clustering, heartbeat and lead profiling per material.

ECG clustering unsupervised hydrogel sensor heartbeat profiling

Advanced Signal Processing Techniques for Disease Biomarkers Discovery

Transforming biomedical signals into informative representations by means of advanced DSP - spectral analysis, time-frequency analysis, higher order statistics, signal decomposition, multi-channel signals. Identifying patterns to use as biomarkers by means of feature engineering and machine learning workflows for enhanced disease prediction and characterization - in Parkinson's motor and non-motor symptoms, and comorbid migraine.

Self-Supervised Graph Representation Learning for In-the-Wild Wearable and Smartphone based Emotion Recognition

Ziogas, I.; Hadjileontiadis, L.J.; Khandoker, A.H.; AlShehhi, A.

Casts in-the-wild wearable/smartphone emotion recognition as inductive graph node classification, training on sampled subgraphs with self-supervised masking and pseudo-labeling tasks to exploit unlabeled data. On the K-EmoPhone dataset, this matches or beats full-label supervised training while using only 20–25% of the labels, improving arousal and valence accuracy by 4.3% and 7.8% respectively.

Artificial Intelligence Review, 2025

Speech Emotion Recognition in Conversations Using Artificial Intelligence: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

Alhussein, G.†; Ziogas, I.†; Saleem, S.†; Hadjileontiadis, L.J. († co-first authorship)

Systematically reviews speech emotion recognition in conversations (2010–2023), screening 1,932 records down to 51 studies for qualitative review and 27 for a meta-analysis of diagnostic test performance. Finds that unimodal speech significantly outperforms multimodal fusion and that deep features edge out hand-crafted ones, and closes with best-practice recommendations given widespread reporting and reproducibility gaps.
